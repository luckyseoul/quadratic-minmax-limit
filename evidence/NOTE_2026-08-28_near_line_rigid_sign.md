# Rigid-sign exclusion for the near-line boundary

Date: 2026-08-28. This is Proposition 15.671. It excludes one product-sign
branch of the first general infinity-present floor survivor by coefficient
arithmetic, without a finite boundary census. The opposite sign and
noncollinear boundaries remain open.

Let the odd-degree boundary of a residual candidate be infinity together
with `p-2` finite points on one affine line. The line direction has `b=1`;
each of the other `p` directions has `b=p-2`, with the two even fibres
corresponding to the two omitted points of the line.

For `p=3 mod 4,c_H=+1`, every directional parity baseline costs `p+1`, so
both quadratic-type budgets are saturated. For
`p=1 mod 4,c_H=-1`, the type containing the line direction has only two
units of surplus and the other type is saturated. From `p=13` onward a
nonzero nonnegative quadratic lift costs at least four units by Proposition
15.642, so the two-unit surplus cannot be used. Thus all slacks are
pointwise rigid in both cases.

On the middle slice, write `z_s=2x_s-1`. The special and transverse targets
are

```
eps_0 S_H = 4 + sigma z_j,
eps_d S_H = 4 - z_a z_b.
```

If `I` is the number of infinity edges and `P_d` the number of finite edges
parallel to direction `d`, coefficient comparison gives, for
`q=(p-1)/2`,

```
P_0 = 4+sigma-I (mod q),
P_d = 4-I       (mod q).
```

Every finite edge is parallel in one direction, so summing gives
`I=3+sigma (mod q)`. The signed inter-fibre `l1` capacities also give

```
2I + (p+1)P_0 <= 8p+1+sigma,
2I + (p+1)P_d <= 8p+2.
```

If `p=1 mod 4`, then `q` is even and `sigma=-1`; hence `I=2 mod q` is even,
contradicting the odd degree of infinity.

If `p=3 mod 4`, write

```
I=4+q k0,       P_0=1+q a_0,       P_d=q a_d.
```

The edge count gives `k0+a_0+sum a_d=8`, while the `l1` bounds give
`k0+(q+1)a_0<=7` and `k0+(q+1)a_d<=8`. For `p>=19`, `q>=9`, so every `a`
vanishes. The edge count forces `k0=8`, contradicting that `I` odd makes
`k0` odd.

Therefore this boundary is impossible for `c_H=-1` when
`p=1 mod 4,p>=13`, and for `c_H=+1` when `p=3 mod 4,p>=19`. This does not
exclude the opposite product sign, noncollinear boundaries, residual (ii),
R1, or the limit.

## Literature and OEIS check

Targeted searches for Paley affine boundaries, odd-fibre Radon profiles,
Johnson-slice xor quadratics, and additive inter-fibre coefficient matrices
found no theorem matching this rigid-floor plus global-parallel-count
argument. Large-arc/conic containment theorems are adjacent finite geometry,
but they do not enter this proof: the boundary here is already a punctured
line, not an arc.

The two displayed prime lists are merely the standard primes in the residue
classes `1 mod 4` and `3 mod 4` (OEIS A002144 and A045326). The cutoff at 13
or 19 comes from the lift-cost and `q>=9` inequalities, not from a new
integer sequence. No OEIS contribution is claimed.

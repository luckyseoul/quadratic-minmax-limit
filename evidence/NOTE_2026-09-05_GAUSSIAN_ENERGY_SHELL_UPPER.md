# Actual Gaussian paired norm: an energy-shell upper bound

2026-09-05. **Direct upper comparison and an explicit shell-width deficit;
the required optimized order comparison remains open.**

Let A be an n by n complete symmetric zero-diagonal signing, n>=2.
Write its extreme eigenvalues as a>0 and -b<0, and put

\[
 \kappa=2/\pi,\quad b_0=1-\kappa,\quad
 \alpha\in[-b,a],\quad \mu=ab+\alpha(a-b),
\]
\[
 H=A\otimes A-\alpha(A\otimes I+I\otimes A),\qquad
 C=I+\kappa H/\mu.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

The actual opposite-phase choice is included, but alpha need not be
generated by a Gibbs temperature for this Gaussian comparison. Let
vec(Z) be centered Gaussian with covariance C, using column vectorization.
Set Lambda=||C||op and

\[
 \mathcal M_A(Z)=\max_{x,y\in\{-1,1\}^n}
       |Q_A(x)-Q_A(y)+x^TZy|,\qquad Q_A(x)=x^TAx/2.
\]

For each attained energy h define

\[
 S_h=\{x:Q_A(x)=h\},\qquad
 T_h=v_h I+t_h A,
\]
\[
 v_h=n-2\kappa\alpha h/\mu,
 \qquad t_h=\kappa(2h-\alpha n)/\mu,
 \qquad w(S,T)=\mathbb E\max_{x\in S}g_T^Tx,
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

where g_T is centered Gaussian with covariance T. Let d_A be the number
of attained energies. Then

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathbb E\mathcal M_A(Z)
 \le \max_{h,k}\bigl[|h-k|+w(S_h,T_k)+w(S_k,T_h)\bigr]
       +n\sqrt{2\Lambda\log(2d_A^2)}.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{3}
\]

In particular d_A<=binom(n,2)+1. When Lambda is bounded, the explicit
remainder is O(n sqrt(log n))=o(n^(3/2)). This is the actual expected
maximum, not an annealed partition bound or a frozen-posterior derivative.

## 1. Positive tensor factorization

Put

\[
 P=(A+bI)/(a+b),\qquad Q=(aI-A)/(a+b).
\]

Both matrices are positive semidefinite and P+Q=I. Since
mu-alpha^2=(a-alpha)(b+alpha)>=0 and mu is a positive affine
interpolation between b^2 and a^2, direct expansion gives

\[
 \Sigma:=I+H/\mu
 =c_P P\otimes P+c_Q Q\otimes Q,
\]
\[
 c_P=(a+b)(a-\alpha)/\mu,\qquad
 c_Q=(a+b)(b+\alpha)/\mu.                                  \tag{4}
\]

Thus C=b_0 I+ kappa Sigma is positive definite. Write the three
positive tensor summands as

\[
 C=\sum_{R\in\{I,P,Q\}}\omega_R R\otimes R,
 \qquad (\omega_I,\omega_P,\omega_Q)
       =(b_0,\kappa c_P,\kappa c_Q).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]

For x in S_h, all three numbers r_h(R)=x^TRx are constant on that
shell: they are n, (2h+bn)/(a+b), and (an-2h)/(a+b).
Consequently the covariance of Zy for any y in S_k is exactly

\[
 \sum_R\omega_R r_k(R)R=T_k.                               \tag{6}
\]

In particular T_k>=b_0 n I and v_k>=b_0 n>0. Formula (6), or a direct
contraction of (1), gives the two coefficients in (2).

For comparison with the bounded-operator setting, if ||A||op<=K sqrt(n),
then ab>=n-1. At the alpha endpoints Sigma equals respectively
(I+A/b) tensor (I+A/b) and (I-A/a) tensor (I-A/a). Formula (4) gives
their convex-combination weights as b^2(a-alpha)/((a+b)mu) and
a^2(b+alpha)/((a+b)mu), respectively. Hence for n>=2,

\[
 \Lambda\le b_0+\kappa(1+2K^2)^2.                           \tag{7}
\]

There is also a source-independent choice: alpha=(a-b)/2 gives
mu=(a^2+b^2)/2 and ||Sigma||op=(a+b)^2/(a^2+b^2)<=2, by its
four spectral-corner values. Thus Lambda<=1+kappa and the remainder
in (3) is universally O(n sqrt(log n)), even without an operator cap
on A. This balances the covariance spectrum; it does not assert
optimality of that alpha, or of an endpoint alpha, for (3).

## 2. Exact comparison on one pair of shells

Fix h,k. On S_h times S_k consider X_(x,y)=x^TZy. Its variance is
the constant V=sum_R omega_R r_h(R)r_k(R). Compare it with

\[
 Y_{(x,y)}=g_{T_k}^Tx+\widetilde g_{T_h}^Ty,
                                                               \tag{8}
\]

using independent Gaussian vectors in (8). For two indices (x,y) and
(x',y'), put s_R=x^TRx' and u_R=y^TRy'. Direct subtraction gives

\[
 \mathbb E(Y_{(x,y)}-Y_{(x',y')})^2
 -\mathbb E(X_{(x,y)}-X_{(x',y')})^2
 =2\sum_R\omega_R(r_h(R)-s_R)(r_k(R)-u_R)\ge0.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]

Each factor is nonnegative by positive semidefiniteness and Cauchy-Schwarz;
zero sector radii cause no exception. This retains the original positive
tensor factors rather than replacing H by its absolute value.

For completeness, the finite Gaussian maximum comparison used in (9)
follows by adding one common independent Gaussian of variance V to every
X index. This changes no expected maximum and makes its point variances
equal to those of Y. Its covariance minus that of Y is
sum_R omega_R(r_h-s_R)(r_k-u_R), entrywise nonnegative with zero diagonal.
Gaussian interpolation of the finite soft maximum then has nonnegative
derivative toward Y: its derivative is one half the soft-max inverse
temperature times the posterior-weighted sum of these covariance
differences. Pass the inverse temperature to infinity. Therefore

\[
 \mathbb E\max_{S_h\times S_k}x^TZy
 \le\mathbb E\max_{S_h\times S_k}Y_{(x,y)}
 =w(S_h,T_k)+w(S_k,T_h).                                   \tag{10}
\]

The two maxima separate exactly in the last equality.

## 3. Combining the shells costs only a subleading term

For each sign sigma and attained h,k, the function

\[
 f_{\sigma,h,k}(Z)=\max_{S_h\times S_k}\sigma x^TZy
\]

is n sqrt(Lambda)-Lipschitz as a function of the standard Gaussian
vector from which Z is generated: every coefficient vector y tensor x
has Euclidean norm n. Gaussian concentration in moment-generating form
therefore gives

\[
 \log\mathbb E\exp\{s(f_{\sigma,h,k}-\mathbb Ef_{\sigma,h,k})\}
 \le s^2 n^2\Lambda/2.                                    \tag{11}
\]

One direct proof of (11) is the heat martingale for a Lipschitz function
of a standard Gaussian. Heat averaging preserves its gradient bound;
the martingale integrand has norm at most that bound, and its exponential
martingale gives (11). Smooth approximation covers finite maxima.
No independence between the different shell maxima is required.

Apply the elementary exponential maximum bound to the 2d_A^2 centered
variables in (11), then optimize s. Their expected maximum is at most
n sqrt(2 Lambda log(2d_A^2)). Both sigma choices have the same mean in
(10), by Gaussian sign symmetry. Adding sigma(h-k) and maximizing gives
(3). This also handles the absolute value in the original objective.

## 4. An explicit constrained-width deficit

The following bound is useful independently of the comparison above.
Let T=vI+tA be positive semidefinite with v>0, and let g have covariance T.
Define

\[
 m(T)=\mathbb E Q_A(\operatorname{sign}g)
      ={n(n-1)\over\pi}\arcsin(t/v),\qquad R=(a+b)/2.
                                                               \tag{12}
\]

Indeed every off-diagonal Gaussian correlation is (t/v)A_ij;
the two-coordinate arcsine identity and A_ij^2=1 give (12).
The scalar argument belongs to [-1,1]. For an attained energy h put

\[
 r={|h-m(T)|\over nR}\in[0,1],\quad
 \delta={1-\sqrt{1-r^2}\over2},\quad
 q_\delta=\Phi_{\rm Gauss}^{-1}((1+\delta)/2).
                                                               \tag{13}
\]

Then the actual constrained width obeys

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 w(S_h,T)\le n\sqrt{2v/\pi}
                      \bigl(2e^{-q_\delta^2/2}-1\bigr).
 \quad}                                                       \tag{14}
\]

In particular, the simpler all-parameter bound is

\[
 w(S_h,T)\le n\sqrt{2v/\pi}
       -n\sqrt{\pi v/2}\,\delta^2
 \le n\sqrt{2v/\pi}
       -{\sqrt{\pi v/2}\,|h-m(T)|^4\over16R^4n^3}.
                                                               \tag{15}
\]

There is no concentration or independence assumption on the signs of g.

To prove these statements, choose a measurable maximizing x(g) in S_h,
write s=sign(g), and let D be their Hamming distance. Subtracting the
scalar matrix (a-b)I/2 does not affect a difference of Boolean quadratic
energies. Its remaining operator norm is R. Since x-s and x+s are
orthogonal, with lengths 2 sqrt(D) and 2 sqrt(n-D),

\[
 |h-Q_A(s)|\le2R\sqrt{D(n-D)}.
\]

Thus D/n>=j((h-Q_A(s))/(nR)), where
j(u)=(1-sqrt(1-u^2))/2 is convex on [-1,1]. Jensen gives E D>=n delta.
The pointwise loss from the unconstrained field maximum is

\[
 \sum_i|g_i|-g^Tx(g)=2\sum_i|g_i|1_{x_i\ne s_i}.
\]

For any q>=0 this is at least
2 sqrt(v) q D-2 sum_i(sqrt(v)q-|g_i|)_+.
Every marginal g_i/sqrt(v) is standard Gaussian, so taking expectations
gives a lower loss of

\[
 2n\sqrt v\,[q\delta-\mathbb E(q-|G_0|)_+].                 \tag{16}
\]

Choose q=q_delta. Since P(|G_0|<=q)=delta, the bracket in (16) is
sqrt(2/pi)(1-exp(-q^2/2)). Also E sum_i|g_i|=n sqrt(2v/pi).
This proves (14). Alternatively,
E(q-|G_0|)_+<=sqrt(2/pi)q^2/2. Optimizing this quadratic lower bound
in (16) gives the first inequality in (15); use delta>=r^2/4 for the
second.

## Scope and the live gap

Equations (3) and (14) are rigorous upper bounds for the actual model.
They do not establish the required bound by 2 sqrt(2) Phi(A), even for
an original-norm near-minimizer. In particular the splitting in (8) has
the ordinary two-field Gaussian-width cost on central-energy shells;
the explicit deficit vanishes when h=m(T). One must improve that
comparison or prove additional information about the actual optimized
source. Neither source optimality nor a solution of the original limit
problem follows from these estimates alone.

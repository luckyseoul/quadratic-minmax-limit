# The integral Paley eigenspace lattice is glued by the profile kernels

Date: 2026-08-24.  Proposition 15.629.  This is an exact structural result
toward R1; it is not the R1 inequality and flips no leftover predicate.

Let (C) be the symmetric Paley conference matrix on
(P^1(\mathbb F_{p^2})), put (m=(p+1)/2), and define

\[
L=\ker_{\mathbb Z}(C-pI),\qquad \operatorname{rank}L=(p^2+1)/2.
\]

For the (m) square projective directions choose linear forms (t_j).
For every (s\in\mathbb F_p), the circle/line word

\[
v_{j,s}=1_{\{\infty\}\cup\{x:t_j(x)=s\}}
\]

lies in (L).  Let (A) be the lattice generated by all (v_{j,s}).

## Quotient theorem

For (x\in L), let

\[
\sigma_j(s)=\sum_{t_j(u)=s}x_u.
\]

The eigenspace reconstruction identity is

\[
p x(u)=\sum_j\sigma_j(t_j(u))-(m-1)x_\infty.
\]

Reduce each profile modulo (p) and write its reduced polynomial as

\[
\bar\sigma_j(s)=\sum_d c_{j,d}s^d.
\]

The reconstruction identity is constant modulo (p), so for every
(d\ge1),

\[
\sum_j c_{j,d}t_j^d=0.
\]

The (m) powers of distinct projective linear forms have rank
(\min(m,d+1)).  Thus only (1\le d\le m-2) occur, with kernel dimension
(m-d-1).  Circle words have constant residue profiles, so they map to zero.
Conversely, lift any tuple in one of these kernels to integer functions
(\rho_j(s)).  Both

\[
\sum_j\rho_j(t_j(u))\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
\sum_{j,s}\rho_j(s)\equiv0\pmod p
\]

hold, and therefore

\[
x={1\over p}\sum_{j,s}\rho_j(s)v_{j,s}
\]

is an integral vector in (L) with the prescribed nonconstant profile
coefficients.  If all such coefficients vanish, the reconstruction identity
expresses (x) directly as an integer combination of the circles.  Hence

\[
L/A\cong\bigoplus_{d=1}^{m-2}
\ker\left[(c_j)\longmapsto\sum_jc_jt_j^d\right]
\]

over (\mathbb F_p), and

\[
[L:A]=p^{\binom{m-1}{2}}.
\]

This is exactly the family of coefficient kernels in Proposition 15.588.
The hard cross-direction Boolean coupling and the (p)-primary lattice glue
are the same object, not two analogies.

## Determinant

Let (B\subset A) be generated by one circle and all within-pencil
differences.  Write

\[
c=e_\infty+{1\over p}1_{\mathbb F_{p^2}},\qquad
r_{j,s}=v_{j,s}-c.
\]

Then (\|c\|^2=2), (c\perp r_{j,s}), different direction blocks are
orthogonal, and each block of differences is (\sqrt p A_{p-1}).  Therefore

\[
\det B=2p^{mp}.
\]

The parallel-class relations show ([A:B]=p^{m-1}).  Combining the two
indices gives

\[
[L:B]=p^{\binom m2},\qquad
\boxed{\det L=2p^{m^2}}.
\]

## Dual, discriminant group, and level

Let

\[
P={1\over2}(I+C/p)={pI+C\over2p}
\]

be the orthogonal projector onto the rational (+p)-eigenspace.  The lattice
(L=V_+\cap\mathbb Z^n) is primitive.  Consequently every integral linear
form on (L) extends to (\mathbb Z^n), and restriction gives

\[
L^*=P\mathbb Z^n.
\]

It follows at once that (2pL^*\subseteq L).  On the other hand, (Pe_i) has
diagonal coordinate (1/2) and every off-diagonal coordinate
(\mathord\pm1/(2p)); hence its class has exact order (2p).  The discriminant
group has order (2p^{m^2}), so its odd primary part is elementary and

\[
\boxed{L^*/L\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\oplus
                 (\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{m^2}}
\]

or, in Smith form,

\[
\mathbb Z/(2p)\mathbb Z\oplus
(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{m^2-1}.
\]

The circle lattice (A) is even and has odd index in (L), so (L) is even.
The candidate level (2p) fails because
(\lVert Pe_i\rVert^2=1/2), making (2p\lVert Pe_i\rVert^2=p) odd.  Conversely,
for (x=Pz),

\[
4p\lVert x\rVert^2
=2\bigl(p\lVert z\rVert^2+z^TCz\bigr)\in2\mathbb Z.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{level}(L)=4p}.
\]

PARI/GP `matkerint` independently certifies the saturated integer kernel at
(p=3,5,7,11).  The exact Smith forms, dual denominators (2p), and levels
(4p) agree with the general theorem above; the PARI calculations are audits,
not the proof.

## Consequence and limit

Prop. 15.589 H identified Max+ as the first shell of an odd coset of (L).
The present result identifies every glue coordinate controlling that shell.
It explains why ordinary minimum-shell and generic modular-lattice theorems
miss R1: (L) has shorter norm-(p+1) circle vectors, while the odd shell is
selected by simultaneous profile relations.  A closing R1 proof must bound
the degree-four harmonic contribution of these coupled glue classes.  That
bound remains open.

## Nearby literature and search audit

Böttcher--Fukshansky--Garcia--Maharaj--Needell study the lattice generated by
the vectors of a rational equiangular tight frame.  In the present notation
that generated lattice is exactly (P\mathbb Z^n=L^*), so their conference-ETF
framework is genuinely adjacent.  Their paper does not identify the integral
kernel (L), its polynomial profile glue, or this discriminant/level formula.
The Paley eigenfunction papers of Goryainov and collaborators classify and
construct support-minimal eigenfunctions; they likewise do not give the
odd-coset shell moment required by R1.

Exact searches for the nontrivial index (25937424601=11^{10}) and for the
closed formulas found only generic prime-power occurrences in OEIS, not this
lattice sequence.  The level (4p) also shows why ordinary unimodular-shadow
theory does not apply verbatim: this is a growing-level vector-valued theta
problem, although the new dual description makes that route precise enough
to investigate rather than invoke heuristically.

# Complete collinear near-line exclusion

Date: 2026-08-28. This is Proposition 15.672. Together with Proposition
15.671, it excludes both product signs when the boundary is infinity plus
`p-2` collinear finite points, for every odd prime `p>=13`. Noncollinear
boundaries and the full non-Walsh remainder remain open.

Take the product sign opposite Proposition 15.671. The special line
direction has one odd boundary fibre and every transverse direction has
`p-2`; the transverse parity baseline is the xnor of the two omitted fibres
and has scaled mean `p-1`.

If `B` is a nonzero lift above a parity baseline, Proposition 15.642 gives

```
E[B] >= (p^2-1)/(16p(p-2)).
```

Its scaled excess is `4p E[B]`. Four lifts therefore cost at least
`(p^2-1)/(p-2)>p+1`, more than either type surplus. There are at most three
nonbaseline directions per type, so every type has a transverse baseline.

Let `I` count infinity edges, `P_d` count finite edges parallel to direction
`d`, and `T` be the total Paley-signed sum of all finite selected edges. The
exact directional mean is

```
a_d = I + (p+1)P_d - eps_d*T - 3p.
```

Thus means in the same quadratic type differ in multiples of `p+1`. The
split budgets force exactly one exception per type, with `a_d=2p` and
parallel count one above the type baseline. When `p=1 mod 4`, the special
line direction itself is the exception in its type.

Write `x,y` for the positive/negative baseline parallel counts and
`m=(p+1)/2`. There are two exceptional edges beyond the baselines, so

```
E=m(x+y)+2,
I=4p-1-m(x+y),
x+y<=7.
```

Every type has a transverse xnor baseline. Its coefficient comparison gives

```
q | I+x-4,     q | I+y-4,     q=(p-1)/2.
```

Putting `p=2q+1,m=q+1` into the edge count turns these into

```
q | y+1,       q | x+1.
```

Hence `x+y>=2q-2`. In the applicable ranges `q>=5`, so this is at least
eight, contradicting `x+y<=7`. The opposite sign is impossible for
`p=3 mod 4,p>=11` and for `p=1 mod 4,p>=13`. Proposition 15.671 supplies
the other sign, proving complete collinear exclusion for every prime
`p>=13`.

modulo-four prime classes, not a new OEIS sequence or submission target.

# Cohomology and topological audit of the signed Boolean Radon fibre

**Status:** rigorous inapplicability verdict for additive cohomology,
Borsuk--Ulam, Tverberg, and oriented-matroid data alone; residual (ii)
remains open.

Let `E=Z^N` be the edge lattice, let `A_ord` be the ordinary compatibility
lattice of Proposition 15.760, and let

\[
 R:E\longrightarrow A_{\rm ord}
\]

be the unsigned edge-Radon map.  Source and target diagonal signings do not
change any of the conclusions below.  This note audits the exact live
question

\[
 (z_0+\ker_{\mathbb Z}R)\cap\prod_e\{0,\tau_e\}\ne\varnothing. \tag{1}
\]

It does not use an analogy between the finite affine plane and anabelian
geometry.

## The complete additive obstruction class

Put the two-term complex

\[
 C^0=E\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{R}C^1=A_{\rm ord}.
\]

Its degree-one cohomology is, by definition,

\[
 H^1(C)=\operatorname{coker}R
       \cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{S(p)}.                    \tag{2}
\]

For a target `y`, its canonical additive lifting obstruction is

\[
 o(y)=[y]\in H^1(C).                                           \tag{3}
\]

Proposition 15.760 proves that the moment map of Proposition 15.759 induces
an isomorphism from (2) to `F_p^{S(p)}`.  Consequently

\[
 o(y)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{all recorded moment congruences vanish}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y\in R(E).                     \tag{4}
\]

This exhausts every additive obstruction, not just the displayed choice of
moments.  Indeed, for every abelian group `G`, any homomorphism
`phi:A_ord -> G` satisfying `phi R=0` factors uniquely through
`A_ord/R(E)`.  Thus after (4), every `H^1`-style linear invariant vanishes.
The complex has no terms in degree at least two, so it supplies no higher
linear lifting class.

There is also no residual additive `Z/2` class of the sort normally used by
antipodal index arguments, since

\[
 H^1(C)\otimes\mathbb F_2=0
\]

for odd `p`.  This is the cokernel form of Proposition 15.757's binary-image
theorem, after its ordinary boundary and total parities have been imposed.

This is the precise cohomological content of Proposition 15.760.  The map
`R` itself is not unimodular.  Indeed, `A_ord` is primitive in the ambient
target.  If `R` were totally unimodular, a nonzero maximal-rank minor would
be `+-1`, making its column lattice saturated in
`A_ord`; this contradicts the nonzero `p`-torsion quotient (2).  What is
unimodular is only the diagonal source/target sign transport.  Once (4)
holds, the remaining obstruction in (1) is an order/box restriction, not a
further class in the group completion.

An exact algebraic invariant of that restriction is the squarefree fibre
coefficient.  In the group algebra of the target lattice, put

\[
 F_R(X)=\prod_{e\in E_{\rm geom}}(1+X^{R(\tau_e e)}).
\]

Then

\[
 [X^y]F_R(X)
 =\#\{x\in\{0,1\}^N:R\operatorname{diag}(\tau)x=y\}.           \tag{5}
\]

Equation (1) is exactly the positivity of (5).  The moment class determines
only whether `y` lies in the group generated by the monomials.  It does not
determine whether the squarefree coefficient (5) is nonzero.  In toric
language this is a squarefree semigroup/Hilbert-function question, strictly
stronger than lattice saturation.

## Why central symmetry and Borsuk--Ulam do not give (1)

Put `A=R diag(tau)` and write the Boolean variable as `x=(1+s)/2`.  The
required equation becomes

\[
 As=2y-A\mathbf1.                                             \tag{6}
\]

The linear map `s -> As` is odd, but the affine map whose zero is needed is
odd only at the centered target

\[
 2y=A\mathbf1=R\tau.                                         \tag{7}
\]

In fact (7) is impossible for every integral edge-Radon target.  Each
off-diagonal row `K_L(s,t)` contains exactly `p^2` edge columns.  Hence its
coordinate in `R tau` is a sum of an odd number of signs and is odd, whereas
the same coordinate of `2y` is even.  Equivalently, the complement
involution sends the fibre over `y` to the distinct fibre over `A1-y`, not
back to itself.

Even at (7), Borsuk--Ulam applied to the continuous sphere would produce an
arbitrary real kernel vector.  It does not force a zero at a sign vertex
`s in {+-1}^N`.  In the actual affine level (6), antipodal equivariance is
absent altogether.  The central symmetry of the *linear kernel* therefore
does not make the Boolean fibre centrally symmetric.

Even abstract central symmetry would not suffice.  For
`A=(1,1,1,5)` and `b=4`, one has `2b=A1`, the real centre
`(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)` is feasible, and `(4,0,0,0)` is an integral lift.
Nevertheless no subset of the columns sums to four, so the Boolean fibre is
empty.

The discrete-cube antipodal theorem does not bridge this gap either.
Oleszkiewicz's theorem replaces antipodal equality on cube vertices by
optimal norm/discrepancy estimates, rather than forcing an exact prescribed
integer value:

<https://doi.org/10.4064/fm-151-2-189-194>

That conclusion is of the same approximate type already shown insufficient
by Proposition 15.761, and its oddness hypothesis also fails by (7).

The continuous relaxation

\[
 P_y=\{x\in[0,1]^N:R\operatorname{diag}(\tau)x=y\}             \tag{8}
\]

does not repair this.  It is either empty or convex and hence contractible.
Its ordinary homology cannot detect whether it contains a cube vertex.
Total unimodularity would turn an integral nonempty relaxation into an
integral-vertex statement, but (2) shows that the standard total-unimodular
route is unavailable for `R`.

## Oriented matroids and Tverberg

The oriented matroid of the columns controls real sign dependencies and
separation, not the magnitudes needed for a squarefree subset sum.  This
failure persists even if the target is included in the configuration.  The
two augmented rank-one configurations

\[
 (A_1,b)=((1,2),2),\qquad (A_2,b)=((1,3),2)
\]

have the same oriented matroid: all three labeled elements are positive on
the unique rank-one orientation.  Their continuous box fibres are both
closed intervals.  But `A_1 x=2` has the Boolean solution `x=(0,1)`, whereas
`A_2x=2` has the signed integral solution `(-1,1)` and no Boolean solution.
Hence neither the oriented matroid of `(A,b)` nor the topology of its real
box fibre can certify (1).  An oriented-matroid/Farkas certificate could
still disprove the *real* relaxation (8); it cannot promote real feasibility
to a Boolean vertex here.

Topological Tverberg gives intersections of images of disjoint faces, with
an unspecified intersection point and arbitrary barycentric weights.  By
contrast, (5) asks for a prescribed target which is the sum of distinct
columns with every used coefficient exactly one.  Rewriting it as the
equal-weight barycentre `y/h` of `h` selected columns does not supply the
disjoint-face partition, dimension hypotheses, or prescribed intersection
point, and Tverberg does not impose the equal unit weights required by (5).
Thus the theorem has no map whose stated conclusion is (5).  A
representative primary statement is:

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10596>

## Anabelian/IUT verdict

The natural obstruction object here is completely displayed in (2)--(5):
a two-term complex of free abelian groups followed by a squarefree
coefficient in a toric group algebra.  Inter-universal Teichmueller theory
is built for arithmetic-geometric comparison objects attached to
hyperbolic curves, number fields, and their anabelian fundamental groups;
the author's primary-paper index is:

<https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/papers-english.html>

No hyperbolic curve, Galois representation, or nonabelian fundamental-group
lifting problem occurs in (1).  Attaching one artificially would have to
retain all labeled edge columns and the squarefree coefficient (5), so it
would merely re-encode the Boolean problem.  The finite-field circle
geometry may still yield a new combinatorial identity for (5), but neither
anabelian reconstruction nor IUT supplies a theorem converting the vanished
class (4) into positivity of (5).

## The one exact convex lever that survives the audit

Let `a_e` be the columns of `A=R diag(tau)`.  The continuous box image is
the zonotope

\[
 Z_A=A[0,1]^N=\sum_e[0,a_e]
     =\operatorname{conv}\bigl(A\{0,1\}^N\bigr).              \tag{9}
\]

By separation and the zonotope support function,

\[
 y\in Z_A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \lambda\mathbin\cdot y
 \le\sum_e\max\{0,\lambda\mathbin\cdot a_e\}
 \quad\hbox{for every real target covector }\lambda.          \tag{10}
\]

A violated (10) would be an exact, common cross-direction box certificate.
Moreover, equality in (10) forces every feasible lift to have

\[
 x_e=1\quad(\lambda\mathbin\cdot a_e>0),\qquad
 x_e=0\quad(\lambda\mathbin\cdot a_e<0),                     \tag{11}
\]

leaving only the zero-weight columns undecided.  Thus a sequence of exposed
faces with a sufficiently small common zero set could genuinely force a
Boolean lift or a contradiction.  This is ordinary convex duality, not a
homotopy theorem, but it is the only audited framework here whose conclusion
addresses the required simultaneous cross-direction box.  Constructing such
a `lambda` from the centered circle/complement words remains open.

**Verdict.**  The only natural `H^1` obstruction is exactly the already
known moment vector.  It vanishes whenever Proposition 15.760 supplies the
signed integral lift.  Borsuk--Ulam sees only the real kernel and loses
equivariance at the live target; Tverberg and oriented matroids lose the
squarefree unit coefficients.  None of these frameworks currently certifies
the signed Boolean intersection (1).  The exact remaining invariant is the
squarefree fibre coefficient (5), or an equivalent special
box-integrality theorem for this edge-Radon matrix and target.

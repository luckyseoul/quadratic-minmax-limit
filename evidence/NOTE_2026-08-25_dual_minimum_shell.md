# The dual Paley lattice has exactly the ETF minimum shell

Date: 2026-08-25. Proposition 15.630. This is a proved lattice theorem and a
structural advance toward R1. It is not the R1 harmonic inequality and does
not close the global limit.

Let

\[
L=\ker_{\mathbb Z}(C-pI),\qquad P={1\over2}(I+C/p)
\]

for the symmetric Paley conference matrix on
\(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{p^2})\). Proposition 15.629 proved
\(L^*=P\mathbb Z^n\). The result here is

\[
\boxed{\min(L^*)={1\over2},\qquad
       \operatorname{Min}(L^*)=\{\pm Pe_i\},\qquad
       |\operatorname{Min}(L^*)|=2(p^2+1).}
\]

Thus the adjacent unit ETF lattice \(\sqrt2L^*\) has minimum one and no
minimal vectors beyond the signed generating frame.

## Circle-frame identity

Let \(v_{j,s}\) be the square affine-circle words, with
\(j=1,\ldots,r\), \(r=(p+1)/2\), and \(s\in\mathbb F_p\). For
\(x\in L^*\), put

\[
a_{j,s}=\langle x,v_{j,s}\rangle\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad t=\sum_s a_{j,s}=2p x_\infty.
\]

The sum is independent of \(j\). Write

\[
c=e_\infty+{1\over p}{\bf1}_{\mathbb F_{p^2}},\qquad
r_{j,s}=v_{j,s}-c.
\]

The vector \(c\) has norm squared two. The \(r_{j,s}\) in one direction
form a scaled \(A_{p-1}\) simplex frame, distinct direction blocks are
orthogonal, and all blocks are orthogonal to \(c\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\sum_{j,s}a_{j,s}^2=p\|x\|^2+{t^2\over2}.}\tag{1}
\]

## Nonzero common sum

Let \(f_p(t)\) be the minimum of \(\sum_{s=1}^p z_s^2\) over integer
vectors with sum \(t\). If \(|t|=ap+b\), \(0\le b<p\), balancing gives

\[
f_p(t)=(p-b)a^2+b(a+1)^2
\]

and hence

\[
(p+1)f_p(t)-t^2
=pa^2+2ab+b(p+1-b)\ge p\qquad(t\ne0).\tag{2}
\]

Applying the balancing bound to all \(r\) profiles in (1) gives
\(2p\|x\|^2\ge p\), so \(\|x\|^2\ge1/2\).

Equality in (2) occurs only at \(|t|=1\) or \(|t|=p\). At \(|t|=p\),
all profiles are constant \(\pm1\), yielding \(x=\pm Pe_\infty\). At
\(|t|=1\), each profile is a single signed delta. The degree-one glue
condition makes the chosen affine lines concurrent (for \(p=3\), two
transverse lines are automatically concurrent), yielding \(x=\pm Pe_u\).

## Zero common sum: MDS plus Newton

Suppose \(t=0\), so every integer profile \(a_j\) has sum zero. For
\(1\le d\le r-2\), define

\[
\mu_d=\left(\sum_s s^d a_{j,s}\right)_j\pmod p.
\]

Pairing \(x\) with the degree-\(d\) glue lifts from Proposition 15.629
shows

\[
\mu_d\in K_d^\perp,
\qquad
K_d=\ker\left[(c_j)\mapsto\sum_jc_jt_j^d\right].\tag{3}
\]

The code \(K_d^\perp\) is the projective Reed--Solomon
\([r,d+1,r-d]\) code: a nonzero degree-\(d\) binary form cannot vanish at
more than \(d\) of the \(r\) distinct projective directions. If exactly
\(h\) profiles are active, (3) has support at most \(h\), and therefore

\[
\mu_d=0\quad\hbox{for }d<r-h.\tag{4}
\]

For an active profile let \(M_j\) be its positive mass. Its positive and
negative parts are two multisets of size \(M_j\) in \(\mathbb F_p\). If
\(M_j<r-h\), (4) says that their power sums agree through degree \(M_j\).
Here \(M_j<p\), so Newton identities recover equal elementary symmetric
functions and hence equal multisets, contradicting activity. Thus
\(M_j\ge r-h\). For \(h<r\),

\[
\sum_jM_j\ge h(r-h)\ge r-1;
\]

for \(h=r\), the trivial floor \(M_j\ge1\) is stronger. It follows that

\[
\sum_{j,s}a_{j,s}^2
\ge\sum_{j,s}|a_{j,s}|
=2\sum_jM_j\ge2(r-1)=p-1.\tag{5}
\]

Equation (1) now gives \(\|x\|^2\ge(p-1)/p>1/2\). This excludes every
zero-sum vector from the minimum shell and completes the theorem.

## Literature and OEIS audit

Böttcher--Fukshansky--Garcia--Maharaj--Needell's lattice generated by the
unit ETF with Gram matrix \(I+C/p\) is isometric to \(\sqrt2L^*\). Their
2016 paper proves by finite enumeration that the exact minimum shell is the
signed frame for the \((5,10)\) and \((13,26)\) cases, corresponding here to
\(p=3,5\). Their Table 1 explicitly records `?, ?` for the minimum-shell and
minimal-basis questions at \((25,50)\), corresponding to \(p=7\). The
present MDS/Newton proof recovers their two finite results and proves the
statement for the standard Paley family at every odd prime, including that
open minimum-shell entry. It does not separately assert that some subset of
the minimum shell is a lattice basis. Their later tight-frame papers provide the correct
rationality/eutaxy setting but no all-prime minimum-shell theorem located in
this search.

Primary sources:

- A. Böttcher, L. Fukshansky, S. R. Garcia, H. Maharaj, D. Needell,
  *Lattices from equiangular tight frames*, LAA 510 (2016), 395--420,
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.05350>.
- A. Böttcher, L. Fukshansky, *Lattices and quadratic forms from tight
  frames in Euclidean spaces*, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.07735>.
- L. Fukshansky, D. Needell, J. Park, Y. Xin, *Lattices from tight frames
  and vertex transitive graphs*, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02862>.

Exact OEIS searches for `20,52,100,244,340`, its longer subsequences, and the
Paley/ETF object returned no matching object entry. The bare formula
\(2(p^2+1)\) is elementary and is not itself asserted to be a new sequence.

## Limit

This theorem settles the ordinary minimum shell of \(L^*\). R1 is about a
degree-four harmonic average on the first shell of the different odd coset
\(y_0+2L\). Minimum-shell eutaxy supplies its spherical benchmark, but does
not determine the required odd-coset harmonic coefficient. R1 and the global
limit remain open.

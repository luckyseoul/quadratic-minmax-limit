# Growing-degree bivector preordering no-go

## Scope and result

This note continues Proposition 6.5j without changing the live mathematical
target.  It concerns only the natural squared-row encoding of the exact
orientation system

\[
 r_e^2=1,\qquad
 g_s(r)=w_s^2-\langle b_s,r\rangle^2\ge0,
 \qquad s=(x,y),                                           \tag{1}
\]

For the half-cut-neighbor system of Proposition 6.5d the widths are

\[
 b_{xy}={x\wedge y\over2},\qquad
 w^+_{xy}=(\sqrt2-1)M+M-{|Q_A(x)+Q_A(y)|\over2},
 \qquad M=\Phi(A).                                        \tag{2}
\]

For the signed outgoing-half system of Propositions 6.5c and 6.5h, exactly
the same rows occur with

\[
 w^-_{xy}=\sqrt2M-{|Q_A(x)-Q_A(y)|\over2}.                 \tag{2a}
\]

Indeed \(\langle b_{xy},r\rangle=x^TRy/2\); the two displayed choices are
precisely the two critical slab systems already derived in those
propositions.  Everything below applies to either sign, because
\(|Q_A(x)\mathbin\pm Q_A(y)|\le2M\) gives the same lower width
\(w^+_{xy},w^-_{xy}\ge(\sqrt2-1)M\).

Proposition 6.5j proves directly that the full degree-four preordering of
(1) has no refutation for `n>=45`.  The new point here is asymptotic and
strictly stronger in degree: uniform Boolean expectation defeats the full
degree-`2D` preordering.  Here `degree-2D` always means raw polynomial degree
before reduction modulo the Boolean equations `r_e^2=1`; no quotient-degree
or differently encoded hierarchy is covered.  The obstruction holds whenever

\[
 (2D+1)^D\left[
  \left(1+{\kappa\over n-1}\right)^D-1
 \right]<1,
 \qquad
 \kappa={\pi^2\over4(\sqrt2-1)^2}=14.3810675\ldots .       \tag{3}
\]

In particular, every fixed degree is blind for all sufficiently large
orders.  More quantitatively, for each fixed `epsilon>0`, (3) holds when

\[
 D\le(1-\epsilon){\log n\over\log\log n}                  \tag{4}
\]

and `n` is sufficiently large depending on `epsilon`.  Thus this exact
preordering needs degree tending to infinity, and the argument rules out
half-degrees through `(1-o(1)) log(n)/log(log(n))`.

This is a proof-system obstruction, not an orientation construction.  It
does not rule out a different lifted encoding, a stronger hierarchy whose
axioms are not generated by (1), a degree growing faster than (4), or direct
dependent rounding.

## 1. Uniform lower width

Put

\[
 t=(\sqrt2-1)M,
 \qquad m_s=\|b_s\|_2^2.
\]

Proposition 5.2 and the exact bivector support formula give

\[
 t^2\ge {3-2\sqrt2\over\pi^2}n^2(n-1),
 \qquad
 m_s=d_H(x,y)(n-d_H(x,y))\le {n^2\over4}.                  \tag{5}
\]

Since `w_s>=t`, every row satisfies

\[
 {m_s\over w_s^2}\le{\kappa\over n-1}=:\rho_n.           \tag{6}
\]

The extra `A`-dependent width in (2) or (2a) is retained, but the uniform lower
bound (6) is enough for the obstruction.

## 2. A mixed-moment bound on low-degree polynomials

Let `E_0` denote uniform expectation on the Boolean cube of the
`N=binom(n,2)` orientation variables.  Let `q` be a multilinear polynomial
of degree at most `d`, and let

\[
 Z_j=\langle b_j,r\rangle,qquad
 m_j=\|b_j\|_2^2,qquad 1\le j\le\ell .
\]

Hölder with all `ell+1` exponents equal to `ell+1`, followed by
Bonami--Beckner for `q` and Khintchine for each linear form, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_0\left[q^2\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}Z_j^2\right]
 &\le \|q\|_{2(\ell+1)}^2
       \prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\|Z_j\|_{2(\ell+1)}^2\\
 &\le (2\ell+1)^{d+\ell}
       \left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}m_j\right)\mathbb E_0q^2.
                                                               \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]

If `d+k<=D` and `ell<=k`, the last constant is at most

\[
 C_D:=(2D+1)^D.                                            \tag{8}
\]

No independence among the rows `b_j` is used in (7); arbitrary repeated
supports and all Pluecker intersections are allowed.

## 3. Every preordering localizer is positive

Take a square-free set `J` of `k` row inequalities, and suppose
`deg(q)<=D-k`.  Expanding their product and applying (7) to every nonempty
subset `L` of `J` yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_0\left[q^2\prod_{j\in J}g_j\right]\\
 &\quad\ge
 \left(\prod_{j\in J}w_j^2\right)\mathbb E_0q^2
 \left[
 1-C_D\sum_{\varnothing\ne L\subseteq J}
       \prod_{j\in L}{m_j\over w_j^2}
 \right]\\
 &\quad\ge
 \left(\prod_{j\in J}w_j^2\right)\mathbb E_0q^2
 \left[1-C_D\big((1+\rho_n)^D-1\big)\right].              \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]

Condition (3) makes the final bracket strictly positive.  Therefore

\[
 \mathbb E_0\left[\sigma_J\prod_{j\in J}g_j\right]\ge0    \tag{10}
\]

for every sum of squares `sigma_J` satisfying
`deg(sigma_J)+2|J|<=2D`.  Repetitions of a `g_j` cause no issue: even
powers can be absorbed into the square multiplier, leaving the square-free
case above.

Uniform cube expectation is nonnegative on the free SOS term and
annihilates every multiple of `r_e^2-1`.  Applying it to a purported
degree-`2D` preordering identity

\[
 -1=\sum_J\sigma_J\prod_{j\in J}g_j
       +\sum_e h_e(r)(r_e^2-1)                              \tag{11}
\]

would give `-1>=0`, a contradiction.  Hence no identity (11) exists under
(3).

## 4. Degree range

For fixed `D`, the left side of (3) is `O_D(1/n)`, proving the first
claim.  If (4) holds, then

\[
 \log (2D+1)^D=D\log(2D+1)\le(1-\epsilon/2)\log n
\]

for sufficiently large `n`, while

\[
 \left(1+{\kappa\over n-1}\right)^D-1
 =(1+o(1)){\kappa D\over n}.
\]

Their product tends to zero, proving the growing-degree statement.

## Verdict

The precise new information is that moving from degree four to degree six,
eight, or any other fixed degree cannot repair the squared-bivector
preordering.  Even a slowly growing degree remains blind.  A proof of the
orientation theorem must therefore use a genuinely nonlocal hierarchy,
substantially growing degree, a different encoding, or a direct
dependency-aware construction.

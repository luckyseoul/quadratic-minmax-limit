# Two-half random-skew criterion and its central-cut entropy barrier

**Status:** exact probabilistic existence criterion, followed by a proved
first-moment barrier throughout the optimal-signing regime.  Even the ideal
assignment `I=0` has threshold `sqrt(log(2)/2)>1/2`, so refining the energy
distribution cannot save this union bound.  This does not close the
multiplier-two diamond.

Let `A` be an order-`n` signing, put

\[
 Q_A(x)=\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}x_ix_j,\qquad M=\Phi(A),
\]

and let `R` be a skew signing.  The desired diamond, with an allowed
unhalved error `E >= 0`, is

\[
 |Q_A(x)+Q_A(y)|+|x^TRy|\le 2\sqrt2 M+E.             \tag{1}
\]

## 1. Exact two-half coordinates

For Boolean `x,y`, set

\[
 u={x+y\over2},\qquad v={x-y\over2}.
\]

The supports of `u` and `v` partition `[n]`, and their nonzero coordinates
are signs.  Conversely, every such ordered signed complementary pair
`(u,v)` gives a unique Boolean pair `(x,y)=(u+v,u-v)`.  If

\[
 I_A(u,v)=Q_A(u)+Q_A(v),\qquad C_R(u,v)=-u^TRv,
\]

then direct polarization, using symmetry of `A` and skewness of `R`, gives

\[
 I_A={Q_A(x)+Q_A(y)\over2},\qquad
 C_R={x^TRy\over2}.                                  \tag{2}
\]

Writing `Q_A(x)=I_A+J_A` and `Q_A(y)=I_A-J_A` also gives

\[
 |I_A|+|J_A|=
 \max\{|Q_A(x)|,|Q_A(y)|\}\le M.                    \tag{3}
\]

In particular `|I_A| <= M`.  Define the exact internal deficit and the
baseline skew allowance by

\[
 \delta_A(u,v)=M-|I_A(u,v)|,\qquad
 a=(\sqrt2-1)M+{E\over2}.                            \tag{4}
\]

Then (1) is exactly

\[
 |u^TRv|\le a+\delta_A(u,v)                           \tag{5}
\]

for every signed complementary pair.

## 2. Exact first-moment criterion

Choose the upper-triangular entries of `R` independently and uniformly from
`{+1,-1}`, and impose skew symmetry below the diagonal.  If
`k=|supp(u)|` and `h=k(n-k)`, then

\[
 u^TRv\ \buildrel d\over=\ S_h=\varepsilon_1+\cdots+\varepsilon_h, \tag{6}
\]

because every edge crossing the support cut occurs exactly once.  Let

\[
 \operatorname{Tail}_h(g)=\Pr\{|S_h|>g\}.             \tag{7}
\]

Let `Omega_*` be the ordered signed complementary pairs with both supports
nonempty.  The group generated by independent global sign changes of `u`
and `v` and by `(u,v) -> (v,u)` acts freely on `Omega_*`; every orbit has
size eight.  Both the event in (5) and its threshold are constant on an
orbit.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 {1\over8}\sum_{(u,v)\in\Omega_*}
 \operatorname{Tail}_{|\operatorname{supp}u|
 (n-|\operatorname{supp}u|)}
 \bigl(a+\delta_A(u,v)\bigr)<1
 }                                                        \tag{8}
\]

is sufficient for the existence of a skew signing `R` satisfying (1).
Indeed, the left side is the expected number of bad event-orbits, so an
expectation below one forces a realization with none.  No independence
between different event-orbits is asserted or used.

For a more tractable, weaker criterion, Chernoff's inequality gives, for
every `lambda > 0`,

\[
 \operatorname{Tail}_h(g)
 \le 2e^{-\lambda g}(\cosh\lambda)^h.                \tag{9}
\]

Let `x,y` be independent uniform Boolean vectors and set

\[
 Z_A(\lambda)=
 \mathbb E_{x,y}\exp\left[-\lambda\left(
 M-\left|{Q_A(x)+Q_A(y)\over2}\right|\right)\right]. \tag{10}
\]

Using `h <= n^2/4`, enlarging the sum harmlessly by the two empty-support
families, and using the bijection above, (8) follows from

\[
 \boxed{
 4^{n-1}e^{-\lambda a}(\cosh\lambda)^{n^2/4}
 Z_A(\lambda)<1.
 }                                                        \tag{11}
\]

This criterion has a one-form free-energy upper bound.  If

\[
 L_A(t)=\mathbb E_x e^{tQ_A(x)},
\]

then independence of `x,y` and
`e^{lambda |z|} <= e^{lambda z}+e^{-lambda z}` give

\[
 Z_A(\lambda)\le e^{-\lambda M}
 \left(L_A(\lambda/2)^2+L_A(-\lambda/2)^2\right).     \tag{12}
\]

All factors in (8)--(12), including the `1/8`, refer to the unhalved
diamond (1).

## 3. Independent first moment cannot close at the optimal scale

The exact criterion (8), not merely the relaxation (11), cannot hold for an
optimal signing at the known asymptotic scale.  Suppose for clarity that
`n` is even and restrict
the sum in (8) to `k=n/2`.  There are

\[
 {1\over8}{n\choose n/2}2^n
   =\exp\bigl((\log4+o(1))n\bigr)                    \tag{13}
\]

central event-orbits.  Since `delta_A <= M`, their thresholds obey

\[
 a+\delta_A\le\sqrt2 M+{E\over2}.                    \tag{14}
\]

If `M=alpha n^{3/2}` and `E=o(n^{3/2})`, the standard Rademacher moderate
deviation estimate, here with `h=n^2/4`, gives

\[
 \log\Pr\left\{|S_h|>\sqrt2M+{E\over2}\right\}
 =-(4\alpha^2+o(1))n.                                \tag{15}
\]

Consequently the left side of (8) is bounded below by

\[
 \exp\bigl((\log4-4\alpha^2+o(1))n\bigr).            \tag{16}
\]

For an optimal signing, the known asymptotic upper bound gives
`alpha <= 1/2+o(1)`, so (16) is at least

\[
 \exp\bigl((\log4-1+o(1))n\bigr),                    \tag{17}
\]

which diverges exponentially.  Thus even the fictitious most favorable
assignment `delta_A=M`, equivalently `I_A=0`, on every central state
would not make the independent-random-skew first moment work.  The ideal
threshold is `alpha>sqrt(log(2)/2)=0.588705...`, already above `1/2`.
No `A`-dependent near-maximizer-count or partition-function refinement can
repair this expectation bound; the sharper universal partition audit is in
`NOTE_2026-09-02_RANDOM_TOURNAMENT_PARTITION_THRESHOLD.md`.

There is no routine variable-Lovasz-local-lemma repair.  The random
variables used by a nontrivial support cut are precisely its crossing
edges.  Any two nontrivial cuts of a complete graph share an edge: in the
four cells determined by the cuts, their common crossing edges are the two
opposite-cell products, and both products can be empty only if one cut is
trivial.  Hence the standard variable-dependency graph is complete.

The rigorous conclusion is limited but sharp: an existence proof for (1)
must exploit an `A`-dependent structured skew signing, or dependencies much
finer than first moment / the ordinary variable local lemma.  Scalar
calculus in `M-|I|` plus independent random skew signs cannot prove the
multiplier-two ray.

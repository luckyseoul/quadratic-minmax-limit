## 2. Four orientation-independent endpoint forms

Work in the all-finite chart `V=F_13^2`. For an undirected selected edge
`e={u,v}`, put `delta_e=u-v`. The Paley sign is unchanged if `delta_e` is
replaced by `-delta_e`. For a linear functional `L`, define

```text
M_2(L) = sum_e chi(delta_e) (L(u)-L(v))^2,
T_3(L) = sum_e chi(delta_e) (L(u)+L(v)) (L(u)-L(v))^2,
M_4(L) = sum_e chi(delta_e) (L(u)-L(v))^4,
U_4(L) = sum_e chi(delta_e) (L(u)+L(v))^2 (L(u)-L(v))^2.
```

These are genuine homogeneous binary forms of degrees `2,3,4,4` in the
two coefficients of `L`. In particular, `T_3` is not the orientation-
dependent odd difference `(L(u)-L(v))^3`: its squared difference makes the
displayed endpoint expression symmetric in `u,v`.

For an exact normalized positive star centered at `j`, write `t=j+y`.
Its four local sums are

```text
sum_(y!=0) y^2,
sum_(y!=0) (2j+y)y^2,
sum_(y!=0) y^4,
sum_(y!=0) (2j+y)^2 y^2.
```

Every power sum `sum_(y!=0)y^d` vanishes in `F_13` for `1<=d<12`.
Consequently all four displayed sums vanish.

The four exact hard directions are distinct projective points. A nonzero
binary form of degree two or three cannot have four projective roots, so

```text
M_2=0,        T_3=0.                                  (1)
```

Both quartics vanish at the same four points. The space of quartics having
those four roots is one-dimensional, generated by the product of their four
root factors. Hence

```text
U_4=lambda M_4                                  (2)
```

for some `lambda in F_13`, unless `M_4=0`. But `M_4=0`, together with
`M_2=0`, would put every required opposite cell into Proposition 15.740's
infeasible nine-vector relaxation. Therefore `M_4` is nonzero. Its
projective zero set is exactly the four exact directions, `lambda` is
unique, and `M_4(L)` is nonzero in all three elevated and all seven opposite
directions.

If `W^L=epsilon_L K^L` is the normalized local coefficient matrix, both
quartic values acquire the same direction sign. Thus every nonexact cell
satisfies

```text
sum W_st (s-t)^2 = 0,
sum W_st (s+t)(s-t)^2 = 0,
sum W_st ((s+t)^2-lambda(s-t)^2)(s-t)^2 = 0,
sum W_st (s-t)^4 != 0.                               (3)
```

## 3. Affine-label invariance and completeness through degree four

Under a fibre relabeling `s'=a*s+c`, `a!=0`, direct expansion gives

```text
M_2' = a^2 M_2,
T_3' = a^3 T_3 + 2c a^2 M_2,
M_4' = a^4 M_4,
U_4' = a^4 U_4 + 4c a^3 T_3 + 4c^2 a^2 M_2.
```

Therefore (1) and the ratio `U_4/M_4` are independent of the affine fibre
labels.

These forms exhaust the orientation-independent homogeneous endpoint
contractions of degree at most four that vanish on the diagonal. Indeed, a
symmetric homogeneous polynomial `f(s,t)` satisfying `f(s,s)=0` is divisible
by `(s-t)^2`. The resulting spaces in degrees `2,3,4` have dimensions
`1,1,2`, with bases

```text
(s-t)^2;
(s+t)(s-t)^2;
(s-t)^4, (s+t)^2(s-t)^2.
```

Thus a further independent cellwise endpoint contraction must either use
degree at least five or add genuinely common-graph information. The full
cross-direction value code of the quartic below remains active.

## 5. Exact-star collision identity

Normalize edge signs so that the 38 hard-type edges are positive and the 21
opposite-type edges are negative. In an exact `P=5` direction there are 33
positive and 21 negative transverse edges. If `a_c,b_c` count them in a
fibre-pair cell `c`, exactness says

```text
a_c-b_c=1     on the twelve star cells,
a_c-b_c=0     on every other cell.
```

The signed contribution of unordered edge pairs colliding in cell `c` is

```text
C(a_c,2)+C(b_c,2)-a_c b_c
 = ((a_c-b_c)^2-a_c-b_c)/2.
```

Summing gives

```text
(12-54)/2=-21
```

per exact direction, and `-84` over all four. The aggregate number alone is
still the sum of four per-direction identities. It becomes common-graph
leverage only when the same colliding edge pairs are tracked across the four
projections.

If `p_i,s` counts the five parallel hard edges inside fibre `s`, and
`g(v)=deg_+(v)-deg_-(v)`, the same star equation gives

```text
sum_(v:L_i(v)=s) g(v)
 = 1+11*1_(s=j_i)+2p_i,s,       sum_s p_i,s=5.         (4)
```

## 6. The 84-class difference-Radon transform

Let

```text
Omega=(F_13^2 minus {0})/{+/-1},       |Omega|=84,
m_delta=# selected edges with displacement class delta,
f_delta=chi(delta)m_delta.
```

For each of the fourteen projective functionals and each
`a in F_13/{+/-1}={0,...,6}`, define

```text
R_L(a)=sum_(L(delta)=+/-a) f_delta,
q_L(a)=epsilon_L R_L(a).
```

Then `q_L(0)=P_L`; for `a>0`, `q_L(a)` is exactly the cyclic-distance
aggregate of `W^L`.

The quartic is already visible in these rows, with the sign convention

```text
sum_(a=1..6) a^4 q_L(a) = epsilon_L M_4(L),
M_4(L)=c product_(i=1..4) det(L,L_i),       c!=0.
```

Let `B` be the resulting `98 x 84` unsigned incidence matrix. Two distinct
displacement classes in the same projective direction meet only in their
common zero bin. Two classes in different directions have exactly the two
functionals satisfying `L(delta)=+/-L(eta)`. A column has one bin in each of
fourteen functional rows. Therefore

```text
B^T B = 13I+2J-G_parallel,                         (5)
```

where `G_parallel` is block diagonal with fourteen `J_6` blocks. The Gram
entries are respectively `14` on the diagonal, `1` for distinct classes in
one direction, and `2` across directions.

If `r` is the projective direction of `delta`, (5) gives the exact inverse

```text
13m_delta
 = P_r-2 epsilon_r T
   +epsilon_r sum_L epsilon_L q_L(|L(delta)|).       (6)
```

Here `T=sum_e chi(e)`. With hard sign `h`, the branch ledger gives
`T=17h`. Thus (6) is an integrality and nonnegativity constraint on all 84
displacement multiplicities, not merely a moment congruence.

Writing `sigma_L=epsilon_L/h`, it is equivalently

```text
13m_delta=P_r-34sigma_r
          +sigma_r sum_L sigma_L q_L(|L(delta)|).
```

Taking norms in (5), subtracting the fourteen zero bins, and using

```text
sum P_L^2 = 4*5^2+3*6^2+7*3^2 = 271
```

gives

```text
sum_(L,a>0) q_L(a)^2 = 13 sum_delta m_delta^2+36.   (7)
```

Put

```text
C=sum_delta C(m_delta,2).
```

Since `sum m_delta=59`, the right side of (7) is `803+26C`. Each exact
star row has `q=(2,2,2,2,2,2)` and energy 24. Removing four such rows yields
the sharp nonstar identity

```text
sum_(three elevated and seven opposite) ||q_L||^2
 = 707+26C.                                         (8)
```

In particular the ten nonstar energy is at least 707 and is `5 mod 26`.
The matched local rows in Section 4 would contribute only
`3*29+7*68=563`, so those particular cells cannot be glued into one graph.

## 8. Midpoint variables recover the information lost by differences

Every affine edge has unique coordinates

```text
{u,v}={m+delta/2,m-delta/2},       delta modulo +/-.
```

Thus the `C(169,2)=14,196` possible edges are binary variables
`n_m,delta`. The difference transform sees only

```text
m_delta=sum_m n_m,delta.
```

Coefficientwise,

```text
M_2(L) = sum chi(delta)n_m,delta L(delta)^2,
T_3(L) = 2 sum chi(delta)n_m,delta L(m)L(delta)^2,
M_4(L) = sum chi(delta)n_m,delta L(delta)^4,
U_4(L) = 4 sum chi(delta)n_m,delta L(m)^2L(delta)^2.
```

Thus `T_3` and `U_4` are exactly the first and second midpoint moments
missing from the 84 displacement totals. Proposition 15.741 certifies this
parameterization and the 84-class Gram/inverse, but does not assert any
uncertified mesh solver result or a binary completion.

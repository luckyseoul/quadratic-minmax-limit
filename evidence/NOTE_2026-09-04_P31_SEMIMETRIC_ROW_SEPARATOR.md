# Exact real-cone semimetric separation for p31 transverse rows

Date: 2026-09-04

**Status:** implemented the exact real compact-cone dual and a normalized
separation LP.  It returns a fixed linear functional that a GPU center search
can update with one multiply-add per changed cell.  A top hard-row witness
passes the scalar, signed-degree, singleton-cut, and two-label-cut gates but
is separated with exact margin `1/14`.  This is a strict new presolver, not
an integral decomposition theorem and not a closure of residual (ii).

## 1. Why the dual is exactly the semimetric cone

Write

\[
 K(a,b;c)=e_{ab}-e_{ac}-e_{bc}.                           \tag{1}
\]

For a semimetric `d`, the triangle inequality says

\[
 \langle K(a,b;c),d\rangle
 =d_{ab}-d_{ac}-d_{bc}\le0.                              \tag{2}
\]

Conversely, the semimetric cone is cut out by the triangle inequalities and
the nonnegativity inequalities `d_ab>=0`.  The latter normals add no new
generator, because for any third label `c`,

\[
 K(a,c;b)+K(b,c;a)=-2e_{ab}.                              \tag{3}
\]

Thus the real cone generated by all compact atoms is exactly the polar of
the semimetric cone.  A hard residual row `x` is in that real cone if and
only if

\[
                         \langle x,d\rangle\le0           \tag{4}
\]

for every semimetric `d`.  The separate identity

\[
                            \sum_e x_e=-b                 \tag{5}
\]

then pins the total compact-atom weight to `b`.

For an opposite row, let

\[
 A(a,b,c)=e_{ab}+e_{ac}+e_{bc}.
\]

Allowing exactly six positive triangles and a real compact cone gives the
support-function criterion

\[
 \langle x,d\rangle
 \le6\max_{a<b<c}(d_{ab}+d_{ac}+d_{bc})                  \tag{6}
\]

for every semimetric `d`, together with `sum x=18-b`.  This is the exact
real relaxation of the row atom system.  Neither (4) nor (6) asserts that an
integral row has an integral atom decomposition.

## 2. Separation LP

Normalize

\[
                             \sum_{a<b}d_{ab}=1.          \tag{7}
\]

For a hard row, maximize `<x,d>` subject to nonnegativity, all `3*C(31,3)`
triangle inequalities, and (7).  A positive optimum is a strict separator.

For an opposite row, add a variable `t` and the `C(31,3)` inequalities

\[
                     d_{ab}+d_{ac}+d_{bc}\le t,           \tag{8}
\]

then maximize

\[
                             \langle x,d\rangle-6t.       \tag{9}
\]

The sparse constraint matrices are cached.  The implementation replays
normalization, nonnegativity, every triangle inequality, the maximum
perimeter, and the returned objective before accepting a learned cut.

As positive controls:

- eleven copies of one compact atom give hard separation margin exactly
  zero;
- six copies of one positive triangle plus six copies of one compact atom
  give opposite margin `-2.1e-15`, numerical zero.

## 3. A strict exact witness beyond the 496-cut bank

Let `U={0,1,2}` and `V={3,4,5}`.  Give every internal edge of each triangle
`U,V` coefficient `-2`, and give the matching edges `03,14,25` coefficient
`+2`.  Finally add five copies of `K(6,7;8)`.

This top hard vector has `b=11` and satisfies all of the following exactly:

```text
edge sum       -11
positive mass   11
negative mass   22
l1               33
signed degrees   nonpositive and even
31 singleton + 465 two-label cut violations   0
```

Nevertheless,

\[
                              x(\delta U)=6.              \tag{10}
\]

The normalized cut metric

\[
 d={\delta(U)\over |U|(31-|U|)}={\delta(U)\over84}       \tag{11}
\]

is a semimetric of total mass one and gives the exact separating margin

\[
                         \langle x,d\rangle={6\over84}
                         ={1\over14}.                     \tag{12}
\]

The full semimetric LP independently returns margin
`0.0714285714285714` with exactly the 84 crossing weights nonzero.  Therefore
the exact metric oracle is strictly stronger than the current incremental
singleton/two-label bank even on the live hard atom count.

## 4. Learned GPU cut

Once a separating semimetric `d` has been found, freeze its 465 normalized
weights and the right-hand side

\[
 R_d=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\text{hard row},\\
 6\max_{a<b<c}(d_{ab}+d_{ac}+d_{bc}),&\text{opposite row}.
 \end{cases}                                              \tag{13}

The reusable score is

\[
                   \max(0,\langle x,d\rangle-R_d).       \tag{14}

For a cell mutation `x_uv <- x_uv+Delta`, its unhinged left side updates as

\[
                   \langle x,d\rangle
                   \leftarrow\langle x,d\rangle+\Delta d_{uv}.             \tag{15}

Thus separation is expensive only when a new cut is learned; evaluating any
stored cut is one multiply-add per changed row cell.  A practical center
search can start with the 496 combinatorial cuts, invoke the LP on its current
frontier, append each positive-margin metric to a small cut pool, and continue
entirely on the GPU until another candidate reaches zero learned-cut cost.

The explicit witness (11) is rational and needs no floating-point trust.
Arbitrary HiGHS output is retained as a normalized numerical learned cut and
is revalidated to `2e-7`; it is a search cut, while final mathematical
exclusion still requires rationalization or an exact atom infeasibility
certificate.

## 5. Scope

The oracle characterizes fractional compact-cone membership, not the integer
semigroup.  It must not be promoted to integral sufficiency without a
normality theorem.  It also does not enforce that 32 independently feasible
rows arise from one 479-edge graph.
